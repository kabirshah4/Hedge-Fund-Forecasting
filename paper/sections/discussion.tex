\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the null results do and do not show.} Hedge funds' 13F trades
are hard to predict beyond habit, but they are not an outlier. Long-only
managers and individual mutual funds are about as hard to predict in the same
pipeline, aggregation does not significantly change that, and neither an
earlier period nor longer training histories help, while the pipeline does
detect planted signals (Tables~\ref{tab:controls} and~\ref{tab:samples}). As a result,
the contrast with the 71\% reported for mutual funds~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}
gives no evidence about hedge fund secrecy or skill. Adding \firmn{} standard firm
characteristics barely helps, so what remains is mainly the holdings data:
fund-level Morningstar holdings with long histories against public filings,
which our design cannot test. The oracle shows that even a fund's overall direction leaves its
stock choices unpredictable. Unpredicted trades show no consistent extra
return, and the window is too short to rule out an effect as large as the one
for mutual funds. For anyone who models or copies managers from disclosures,
the message is the same for both: expect little beyond habit, and do not
treat unpredicted trades as a reliable signal.

\paragraph{Implications for foundation models in finance.} Position-size
histories are short (a median of \medhistM{} quarters), irregular and driven
by decisions rather than dynamics, so zero-shot models, even one pre-trained
on financial returns, have little to work with; pre-training on holdings
instead of prices is the natural next step.

\paragraph{Two pitfalls.} Keeping a stock's next-quarter return only if it
stays in the top-500 universe, which is known only afterwards, inflated an
early version of our stock-level spread to 0.79\% per quarter. And a fund that
changes legal entity silently drops out of a sample built on one SEC
identifier; we found and fixed one such case (Winton).

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
\textbf{What 13F shows:} long positions at quarter-end, combined
  across a firm; no shorts, derivatives or trading within the quarter.
  \textbf{Samples:} the hand-picked list is curated; the Form ADV list
  misses funds that file under other entity names and does not follow
  reorganizations; three ETF sponsors pass the long-only rule (we report
  results without them).
  \textbf{Scope:} new positions are excluded, as
  in~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, although they may be the most informative
  trades for activist funds; the universe excludes small stocks.
  \textbf{Data and power:} Yahoo prices are missing for 5\% of test
  positions; FNSPID news ends in 2023; RQ3 has 18 quarters.
