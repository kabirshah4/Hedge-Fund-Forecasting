\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Labels.} Following~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, for fund $i$, stock $s$
and quarter $t$ we compare split-adjusted shares $sh_{i,s,t}$ with
$sh_{i,s,t-1}$:
\[
y_{i,s,t} =
\begin{cases}
\text{BUY} & \text{if } sh_{i,s,t} / sh_{i,s,t-1} - 1 \ge 0.01,\\
\text{SELL} & \text{if } sh_{i,s,t} / sh_{i,s,t-1} - 1 \le -0.01,\\
\text{HOLD} & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
so a full exit ($sh_{i,s,t}=0$) is a SELL. We adjust for splits with Yahoo's
split history for stocks with a U.S.\ ticker and with a holdings-based
detector for the rest. The adjustment passes nine hand-checked cases (e.g.\
Apple 4:1 in 2020, NVIDIA 10:1 in 2024, AMD with no split). We form no labels
across a missed filing. At most \splitlikepct\% of prediction cases have a
share ratio within 0.2\% of a split ratio with no split applied. This is an
upper bound on missed splits, since many of these are trades that exactly
double or halve a position. Only \cusipchangen{} test-period exits
(\cusipchangepct\% of SELLs) coincide with a new position in a different CUSIP
of the same issuer, the sign of a CUSIP change.

\paragraph{Prediction task and timing.} As in~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, we
predict $y_{i,s,t}$ for positions held at the end of $t-1$. Features use only
13F reports for quarter $t-1$ and earlier, which are public by 45 days into
quarter $t$, plus prices and indices up to the end of $t-1$. The prediction is
therefore available prior to the due date of the fund's report for quarter $t$. Like
\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, we exclude new positions (zero shares at $t-1$,
\newM\% of labels in the main sample). Predicting \emph{which} of 500 stocks a
fund will start buying is a different and far more imbalanced task, which we
leave to future work. We split the data by time: training 2013Q3--2020Q4,
validation 2021 (early stopping and cut-offs only), and test 2022Q1--2026Q2.

\paragraph{Features.} The fund's last three actions on the stock; holding age;
portfolio weight and rank; the fund's number of holdings, last-quarter
turnover, buy share and long-run average action; the stock's last-quarter
return, four-quarter momentum and size rank; the share of the other funds
\emph{in the same sample} that bought or sold the stock last quarter; and
average policy uncertainty. As a check we add \firmn{} firm characteristics:
book-to-market, earnings yield, ROA, gross profitability, operating margin,
leverage, cash, and asset and sales growth from SEC XBRL statements (used only
if the period ended at least 120 days before the cutoff), and one-month
return, 12--1 momentum, volatility, beta, illiquidity and turnover.

\paragraph{Models.} \emph{Baselines:} each fund's most common training action
(the baseline of~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}); its most common action over the
previous eight quarters; the fund's last action on the stock; and a one-line
rule (BUY if the fund bought more than it sold last quarter).
\emph{Learned models:} multinomial logistic regression with fund dummies;
LightGBM (multiclass, 63 leaves, learning rate 0.05, at least 200 cases per
leaf, feature and row subsampling 0.8, L2 penalty 1, no class weights, early
stopping on 2021, fund identity as a categorical feature); and an LSTM, the
model family of~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}. The LSTM reads the position's last
eight quarters (action, log change in shares, log portfolio weight, held flag)
with one 32-unit layer, and joins the standardized static features and an
8-dimensional fund embedding in a 64-unit head (dropout 0.2, Adam, early
stopping on 2021 log-loss; full settings in the code). All hyperparameters were fixed in advance rather than
tuned; 2021 is used only for early stopping and cut-offs.
\emph{Foundation models, zero-shot:} Chronos-2~\cite{ansari2025chronos2}
(\texttt{amazon/chronos-2}) and TimesFM~2.5~\cite{das2024timesfm} (200M
parameters) forecast the position's split-adjusted share history (up to 32
quarters, normalized to 1 at $t-1$); for Chronos-2 we also add stock returns
and policy uncertainty as covariates. A Chronos model pre-trained from scratch
on U.S.\ stock returns up to 2020~\cite{rahimikia2025tsfm}
(\texttt{FinText/Chronos\_Small\_2020\_US}), which has never seen our
validation or test years, forecasts the position's quarterly log changes.
Each model returns nine quantiles (deciles 0.1--0.9), from which we compute
$P(\text{up}>1\%) - P(\text{down}>1\%)$. Two cut-offs chosen on 2021 turn this
score into a label, and we also evaluate how well the score ranks (AUC).
\emph{Diagnostics:} LightGBM trained with equal total weight per fund, and an oracle that is told each fund's
actual net direction in quarter $t$ (BUY if it bought more of its positions
than it sold), used alone or as an extra LightGBM feature.
\emph{Hybrid and news versions} add to LightGBM either the foundation models'
forecasts or news features (headline count, mean FinBERT sentiment, shares of
clearly positive and negative headlines, change in sentiment). The news comes
from quarter $t-1$ (a forecast) or quarter $t$ (a nowcast, still made before
the 13F report for $t$ is public), averaged over the whole quarter or over its
last 15 days only. We first remove headlines that mention a stake, a 13F, 13D
or 13G filing, or an investor taking, raising, lowering, cutting or exiting a
position (0.7\%), so news that reports a fund's own trade cannot leak into
its prediction. An LLM (Llama~3.1 8B, trained to December 2023) also rates
each stock-quarter's headlines for outlook and new information; to test for
memorization, 2021--2023 is rescored with names hidden~\cite{gao2025lookahead}.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Our main measure is mean per-fund accuracy (the measure
of~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}). We also report pooled accuracy, macro-F1, and
three measures that depend less on the class mix: the share of the
baseline's errors that the model fixes, the reduction in log-loss relative to
each fund's training class frequencies, and the AUC for BUY versus SELL.
Confidence intervals resample whole funds (2,000 draws). To test whether a
gain could be luck, we apply the Diebold--Mariano
test~\cite{diebold1995comparing} to quarterly fund-averaged differences in
accuracy and log-loss (Newey--West, one lag; correction of~\cite{harvey1997testing}).

\paragraph{Leakage audit.} For three test quarters $T$ (2022Q1, 2024Q2,
2026Q1), we replace every input dated in quarter $T$ or later (holdings,
actions, prices, 13F-implied prices, universe ranks, policy uncertainty) with
random values and rebuild the features. The features for quarter $T$ stay the
same in all three cases, while those for $T+1$ change, as they should. We further check that the inputs
were public in time: only 0.03\% of the holdings that feed test-period
features were filed, as original reports or amendments, after the matching
prediction would be made.

\paragraph{Returns (RQ3).} A test-period trade is \emph{predicted} if the
model's label matches the fund's action, and a \emph{surprise} otherwise. The
primary test uses the pre-disclosure window: from the end of quarter $t$ to the
day the fund's 13F for $t$ became public (its first filing, capped at the
45-day deadline). For each trade we take the stock's dividend-adjusted return
over this window minus the average universe stock's return, and average across
trades each quarter. We form three spreads (sells: predicted minus surprise;
buys: surprise minus predicted; stocks: least minus most predictable quintile,
by the share of their holders' trades the model predicted), so a positive
value always means the surprising trades did better. We test the 18
quarterly spreads (2022Q1--2026Q2) with Newey--West standard errors (one lag)
and $t$-distribution $p$-values, and correct for multiple testing with Holm's
method within each sample and across samples. Secondary versions use a size-
and momentum-matched benchmark (same size and momentum tercile, in the spirit
of~\cite{daniel1997measuring}), sort trades by the model's probability of the
action taken (surprise intensity), report all buys minus all sells, compare
surprise and predicted trades within the same fund and quarter, use the
three months after the filing (a copier's window), and relate each fund's predictability to its trades' payoff. We
regress calendar-time portfolios held for 3--12 months on the Fama--French
five factors~\cite{fama2015five} and momentum~\cite{carhart1997persistence},
with Newey--West errors~\cite{newey1987hac} whose lags equal the holding
period. Yahoo returns cover the stocks behind 95\% of test positions. The rest
are mostly stocks without a current U.S.\ ticker, many of them delisted, and
they are spread evenly across buys, sells, surprises and predicted trades. As
a bound, we treat every trade without a price as a delisting that loses 30\%
in the window. Each stock in the universe at quarter-end keeps its later
returns even if it leaves the universe.
