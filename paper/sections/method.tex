\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Labels.} Following~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, for fund $i$, stock $s$
and quarter $t$ we compare split-adjusted shares $sh_{i,s,t}$ with
$sh_{i,s,t-1}$:
\[
y_{i,s,t} =
\begin{cases}
\text{BUY} & \text{if } sh_{i,s,t} / sh_{i,s,t-1} - 1 \ge 0.01,\\
\text{SELL} & \text{if } sh_{i,s,t} / sh_{i,s,t-1} - 1 \le -0.01,\\
\text{HOLD} & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
so a full exit ($sh_{i,s,t}=0$) is a SELL.
Split adjustments use Yahoo's split history for stocks with a U.S.\ ticker and
a holdings-based detector for the rest; the adjustment passes nine
hand-checked cases (e.g.\ Apple 4:1 in 2020, NVIDIA 10:1 in 2024, AMD with no
split). No labels are formed across a missed filing. At most \splitlikepct\%
of prediction cases have a share ratio within 0.2\% of a split ratio with no
split applied, an upper bound on missed splits since many are trades that
exactly double or halve a position, and only \cusipchangen{} test-period
exits (\cusipchangepct\% of SELLs) coincide with a new position in a
different CUSIP of the same issuer, the signature of a CUSIP change.

\paragraph{Prediction task and timing.} As in~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, we
predict $y_{i,s,t}$ for positions held at the end of $t-1$. Features use only
13F reports for quarter $t-1$ and earlier, which are public by 45 days into
quarter $t$, plus prices and indices up to the end of $t-1$; the prediction is
therefore available before the fund's report for quarter $t$ is due. Like
\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, we exclude new positions (zero shares at $t-1$,
\newM\% of labels in the main sample): predicting \emph{which} of 500 stocks a
fund will start buying is a different, far more imbalanced task, and we leave
it to future work. Data are split by time: training 2013Q3--2020Q4, validation
2021 (early stopping and threshold choice only), test 2022Q1--2026Q2.

\paragraph{Features.} The fund's last three actions on the stock; holding age;
portfolio weight and rank; the fund's number of holdings, last-quarter
turnover and buy share, and long-run average action; the stock's last-quarter
return, four-quarter momentum and size rank; the share of the other funds
\emph{in the same sample} that bought or sold the stock last quarter; and
average policy uncertainty.

\paragraph{Models.} \emph{Baselines:} each fund's most common training
action (the baseline of~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}); its most common action
over the previous eight quarters; the fund's last action on the stock; and a
one-line rule (BUY if the fund bought more than it sold last quarter). \emph{Learned models:} multinomial logistic regression with fund
dummies; LightGBM (multiclass, 63 leaves, learning rate 0.05, at least 200
cases per leaf, feature and row subsampling 0.8, L2 penalty 1, no class
weights, early stopping on 2021, fund identity as a categorical feature); and
an LSTM, the model family of~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}, which reads the
position's last eight quarters (action, log change in shares, log portfolio
weight, held flag) with one 32-unit layer and joins the standardized static
features and an 8-dimensional fund embedding in a 64-unit head (dropout 0.2;
Adam, learning rate $2\cdot10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$; batches of 512;
early stopping on 2021 log-loss). All hyperparameters were fixed in advance
rather than tuned; 2021 is used only for early stopping and cut-offs. Per-fund
variants use the same models with smaller capacity (16-unit LSTM, 15 epochs;
logistic regression with $C = 0.5$). \emph{Foundation models,
zero-shot:} Chronos-2~\cite{ansari2025chronos2} (\texttt{amazon/chronos-2})
and TimesFM~2.5~\cite{das2024timesfm} (200M parameters) forecast the
position's split-adjusted share history (up to 32 quarters, normalized to 1 at
$t-1$); for Chronos-2 we also add stock returns and policy uncertainty as
covariates; and
a Chronos model pre-trained from scratch on U.S.\ stock returns up to
2020~\cite{rahimikia2025tsfm} (\texttt{FinText/Chronos\_Small\_2020\_US}),
which has never seen our validation or test years, forecasts the position's
quarterly log changes. Each model returns nine quantiles (deciles 0.1--0.9), which give
$P(\text{up}>1\%) - P(\text{down}>1\%)$; two cut-offs chosen on 2021 map this
score to a label, and we also evaluate the score's ranking (AUC).
\emph{Diagnostics:} LightGBM trained with equal total weight per fund; LightGBM
without the peer features; and an oracle that is told each fund's actual net
direction in quarter $t$ (BUY if it bought more of its positions than it
sold), used alone or as an extra LightGBM feature. \emph{Hybrid and news variants} add to LightGBM the foundation
models' forecasts, or news features (headline count, mean FinBERT sentiment,
shares of clearly positive and negative headlines, change in sentiment) from
quarter $t-1$ (a forecast) or quarter $t$ (a nowcast, still made before the
13F report for $t$ is public), averaged over the quarter or over its last 15
days only. Headlines that mention a stake, a 13F, 13D or 13G filing, or an
investor taking, raising, lowering, cutting or exiting a position (0.7\%) are
removed first, so news that reports a fund's own trade cannot leak into its
prediction.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Mean per-fund accuracy (the measure
of~\cite{cohen2026mimicking}), pooled accuracy and macro-F1, and three
measures that do not depend on the class mix: the share of the baseline's
errors that the model fixes, the reduction in log-loss relative to each fund's
training class frequencies, and the AUC for BUY versus SELL. Confidence
intervals resample whole funds (2,000 draws).

\paragraph{Leakage audit.} For three test quarters $T$ (2022Q1, 2024Q2,
2026Q1) we replace every input dated in quarter $T$ or later (holdings,
actions, prices, 13F-implied prices, universe ranks, policy uncertainty) with
random values and rebuild the features: the features for quarter $T$ are
unchanged in all three cases, while those for $T+1$ change, as they should.
Inputs are also public in time: only 0.03\% of the holdings that feed
test-period features were filed, as original reports or amendments, after the
corresponding prediction would be made.

\paragraph{Returns (RQ3).} A test-period trade is \emph{predicted} if the
model's label matches the fund's action and a \emph{surprise} otherwise. The
primary test uses the pre-disclosure window: from the end of quarter $t$ to
the day the fund's 13F for $t$ became public (its first filing, capped at the
45-day deadline). For each trade we take the stock's dividend-adjusted return
over this window minus the average universe stock's, average across trades
each quarter, and form three spreads (sells: predicted minus surprise; buys:
surprise minus predicted; stocks: least minus most predictable quintile, by
the share of their holders' trades the model predicted), so positive values
always mean that surprising trades did better. The 18 quarterly spreads (2022Q1--2026Q2) are
tested with Newey--West standard errors (one lag) and $t$-distribution
$p$-values, and corrected for multiple testing with Holm's method within each
sample and across samples. Secondary versions
use a size- and momentum-matched benchmark (same size and momentum tercile,
in the spirit of~\cite{daniel1997measuring}), sort trades by the model's
probability of the action taken (surprise intensity), report all buys minus
all sells, compare surprise and predicted trades within the same fund and
quarter, use the quarter of the trade itself and the three months after the
filing (a copier's window), and relate each fund's predictability to its
trades' payoff. Calendar-time portfolios held for 3--12
months are regressed on the Fama--French five factors~\cite{fama2015five}
and momentum~\cite{carhart1997persistence}, with Newey--West
errors~\cite{newey1987hac} whose lags equal the holding period. Yahoo
returns cover stocks behind 95\% of test positions; the rest are mostly
stocks without a current U.S.\ ticker, many of them delisted, and are spread
evenly across buys, sells, surprises and predicted trades. As a bound we
treat every trade without a price as a delisting that loses 30\% in the
window. Each stock in the universe at quarter-end keeps its later returns even
if it leaves the universe.
